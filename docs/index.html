Methodology

�🛠️ Hierarchical Control Architecture

The control system is organized into three layers: a global planner (ROS2 Nav2) generates obstacle-avoiding paths, a local planner (Stanley controller) adapts for skateboard kinematics, and low-level controllers execute steering and pushing via inverse kinematics and joint control. Real-time feedback enables robust adaptation to disturbances and trajectory deviations.

Positioning for Push-Off

To prepare for push-off, the robot moves one leg near the skateboard and shifts its center of mass (CoM) laterally for balance. The swing leg follows a parabolic path, and the CoM shift keeps the robot stable as the leg is repositioned. Both leg and torso motions are mapped to joint angles via inverse kinematics for coordinated movement.

🚀 Velocity-Based Pushing Control

Forward motion is achieved by regulating leg speed during ground contact. The system adapts the leg trajectory based on velocity error, using feedback from foot sensors. Joint commands are smoothed and tracked by PD controllers for precise and stable motion.

🔄 Steering via CoM Shift

Turning is controlled by laterally shifting the robot's CoM, generating torque to steer the skateboard. The turning radius is directly related to CoM displacement, allowing smooth and accurate maneuvering through torque equilibrium and kinematic relationships.

Key Contributions

📊 Novel Control Framework

First comprehensive approach to quadrupedal robot skateboarding with hierarchical control combining MPC trajectory planning and joint-space torque control.

🔬 Scientific Validation

Extensive experimental validation in both simulation and real-world environments, demonstrating robust performance across conditions.

🌐 Open Research

Complete open-source framework enabling reproducible research and further development in sports robotics applications.
